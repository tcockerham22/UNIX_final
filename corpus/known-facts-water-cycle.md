# Smoke-test facts: the water cycle

The water cycle describes the continuous movement of water among the ocean,
land, atmosphere, and living things. Solar energy drives evaporation and
transpiration, while gravity moves water downhill and causes precipitation
to fall. Water changes between liquid, solid, and gas phases as energy is
absorbed or released.

## Evaporation and transpiration

Evaporation occurs when liquid water at a surface changes into water vapour.
It can happen below the boiling point. Higher temperatures, moving air, and
dry conditions can increase evaporation, although local weather and surface
conditions also matter. Water vapour is invisible; the white cloud-like
appearance above a cold drink or kettle is made of tiny liquid droplets or
ice crystals formed after vapour cools.

Transpiration is the release of water vapour by plants, primarily through
small pores in leaves. Evaporation and transpiration together are often called
evapotranspiration. The process transfers water from soils and vegetation to
the atmosphere and also affects local energy balances. Not all rainfall
returns immediately to the air: some runs off, infiltrates soil, or is
temporarily stored.

## Condensation and precipitation

Condensation occurs when water vapour cools and becomes liquid water.
Atmospheric water droplets form around small particles called condensation
nuclei. Clouds consist of many tiny water droplets, ice crystals, or a mix
of both. A cloud does not simply represent a large mass of water vapour; the
visible cloud is composed mainly of condensed liquid and frozen water.

Precipitation is water that falls from clouds to the surface. It includes
rain, snow, sleet, and hail. Whether precipitation reaches the ground as rain
or snow depends on the temperature profile through the atmosphere, not only
the temperature measured at the ground. Some precipitation evaporates before
reaching the surface.

## Infiltration, runoff, and storage

Infiltration is the movement of water into the soil. The rate depends on soil
texture, structure, vegetation, slope, and how wet the soil already is.
Permeable ground can allow water to percolate downward and recharge
groundwater. Impermeable surfaces such as pavement increase the fraction of
rainfall that flows over land as runoff.

Runoff travels across the land and through streams and rivers toward lakes
and oceans. Groundwater can move slowly through pores and fractures in rock.
Aquifers are geological formations that store and transmit groundwater.
Water can remain in reservoirs, glaciers, soils, lakes, or underground for
different lengths of time. The water cycle therefore includes both rapid
flows and long-term storage.

## Human influence and careful interpretation

People change water flows through dams, irrigation, groundwater pumping,
urban development, and land-cover changes. These activities can affect
flooding, water availability, ecosystems, and the timing of river flow.
Climate change alters temperatures and precipitation patterns, but the
effects differ by region and season. A general description of the water
cycle should not be mistaken for a local forecast.

For a smoke test, ask which process changes liquid water into vapour at a
surface. The answer is evaporation, supported by this source. Transpiration
also adds vapour to the atmosphere but specifically describes water released
by plants. A grounded answer should preserve this distinction and cite the
source passage retrieved from this document.
