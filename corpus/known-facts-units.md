# Smoke-test facts: common units and conversions

Measurements are meaningful only when the quantity and unit are both clear.
The International System of Units (SI) provides a shared framework for
measurement. Its base units include the metre for length, kilogram for mass,
second for time, ampere for electric current, kelvin for temperature, mole for
amount of substance, and candela for luminous intensity.

## Length and distance

One metre is exactly 100 centimetres. One kilometre is exactly 1,000 metres.
Therefore, 2.5 kilometres equals 2,500 metres. The centimetre is one
hundredth of a metre, and the millimetre is one thousandth of a metre.
Prefixes such as kilo-, centi-, and milli- represent powers of ten in the SI
system.

An inch is exactly 2.54 centimetres. A foot contains 12 inches, so one foot is
30.48 centimetres. A yard contains three feet and is 0.9144 metres. A
statute mile is 5,280 feet or exactly 1,609.344 metres. These conversions
distinguish customary units from SI units and should not be confused with a
nautical mile, which is a different unit used in navigation.

## Mass, time, and volume

One kilogram is 1,000 grams. One metric tonne is 1,000 kilograms. Mass is not
the same physical quantity as weight: weight is a force caused by gravity,
while mass measures inertia and is expressed in kilograms.

One minute is 60 seconds, and one hour is 60 minutes or 3,600 seconds. One
day is 24 hours by the conventional civil-time definition. Calendar days and
local clock changes can be affected by time zones and daylight-saving
policies, but the SI second remains a fixed unit of time.

One litre is exactly one cubic decimetre. One millilitre is one thousandth of
a litre and equals one cubic centimetre. A US customary gallon is not the same
size as an imperial gallon. A US liquid gallon is approximately 3.785 litres,
while an imperial gallon is approximately 4.546 litres. Always specify which
gallon is intended.

## Temperature

The Celsius and Kelvin scales have equal-sized degree intervals. A temperature
of zero degrees Celsius is 273.15 kelvins. To convert Celsius to Kelvin, add
273.15. Fahrenheit uses a different interval and origin: degrees Fahrenheit
equal nine fifths of the Celsius value plus 32. For example, 0 degrees
Celsius is 32 degrees Fahrenheit, and 100 degrees Celsius is 212 degrees
Fahrenheit.

Temperature differences should be distinguished from absolute temperatures.
A change of one degree Celsius has the same magnitude as a change of one
kelvin. It is not equal to a one-degree Fahrenheit change; one degree Celsius
corresponds to 1.8 degrees Fahrenheit. Use the formula appropriate to the
quantity being converted and retain enough precision for the task.

## Energy and power

The joule is the SI unit of energy. One joule is the energy transferred when
one newton of force acts through one metre in the direction of the force. The
watt is the SI unit of power and equals one joule per second. A kilowatt-hour
is a unit of energy, not power: it is the energy used by a one-kilowatt
device operating for one hour.

One kilowatt-hour equals 3.6 million joules. Electricity bills commonly
report kilowatt-hours because they measure energy over a billing interval.
The rate at which energy is used is power, usually measured in watts or
kilowatts. Confusing energy and power can lead to incorrect comparisons.

## Checking conversions

When solving a conversion, write the starting quantity with its unit and
multiply by a conversion ratio whose numerator and denominator have the same
physical quantity. Arrange the ratio so the original unit cancels. For
example, converting kilometres to metres uses 1,000 metres per kilometre.
Check that the remaining unit is the requested unit, then assess whether the
numerical direction makes sense: converting kilometres to metres should make
the number larger.
