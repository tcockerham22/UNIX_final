# Smoke-test facts: the Solar System

The Solar System consists of the Sun and the objects gravitationally bound to
it. These include eight recognized planets, dwarf planets, moons, asteroids,
comets, and smaller bodies. The Sun contains most of the Solar System's mass.
Its gravity dominates the motions of the planets and many smaller objects.

## Planets in order

In order outward from the Sun, the eight planets are Mercury, Venus, Earth,
Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune. Mercury is the closest planet to
the Sun. Neptune is the most distant of the eight recognized planets. Pluto
is classified by the International Astronomical Union as a dwarf planet, not
as one of the eight planets. A useful smoke-test question is: which planet is
third from the Sun? The answer is Earth, and this document is a suitable
source for that fact.

The four inner planets—Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars—are terrestrial
planets. They have solid surfaces and are relatively small compared with the
giant planets. Jupiter and Saturn are gas giants, dominated by hydrogen and
helium. Uranus and Neptune are often called ice giants because water, ammonia,
and methane compounds are important in their interiors. These categories are
scientific summaries; the planets have complex structures and atmospheres.

## Orbits and distances

Planets travel around the Sun in elliptical orbits. Most planetary orbits lie
close to a shared plane called the ecliptic. An astronomical unit, abbreviated
AU, is defined from the mean Earth–Sun distance and is approximately
149.6 million kilometres. Distances in AU make it easier to compare planetary
orbits: Earth is about 1 AU from the Sun, while the outer planets are many AU
away.

The time a planet takes to orbit the Sun increases with its average distance
from the Sun. Earth's orbital period is about one year. Neptune's period is
roughly 165 Earth years. The rotation period, which measures a body's spin,
is a different quantity from the orbital period. A planet can rotate quickly
or slowly regardless of how long its year is.

The planets do not shine by producing visible light in the way the Sun does.
They are visible because they reflect sunlight. Their apparent brightness
depends on distance, size, reflectivity, and viewing geometry. A moon is a
natural satellite that orbits a planet or another small Solar System body.
Earth's Moon is the fifth-largest natural satellite in the Solar System.

## The Sun and small bodies

The Sun is a star, a hot sphere of plasma whose energy comes from nuclear
fusion in its core. The Solar System formed from a collapsing cloud of gas and
dust about 4.6 billion years ago. Remaining material formed planets and
smaller bodies. The asteroid belt lies mainly between Mars and Jupiter. Many
comets originate in distant reservoirs beyond the major planets.

An asteroid is a small rocky or metallic body. A comet contains volatile
materials that can form a coma and tails when solar heating causes gases and
dust to escape. A meteor is the streak of light produced when a meteoroid
enters an atmosphere. A meteorite is a fragment that survives the passage and
reaches the ground. These terms describe different stages or kinds of object.
